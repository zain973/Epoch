1. Client Understanding: The goal is to provide a digital blogging platform's Minimum Viable Product (MVP). 
We have chosen Track A: The Classic Model in response to the Board of Directors' disagreement regarding user experience. 
Transparency and timeliness will be given top priority on the platform via a Standard Chronological Global Feed. 
In order to ensure a shared community experience free from algorithmic filtering and personalisation bias, every user will see the same content in the order that it was published.


2. Creative Freedoms
"Epoch" is the product name, which highlights the content delivery's chronological, time-based structure.
"Timeline Ribbon" is the UI layout. To support the "Latest First" concept, articles will flow in a vertical stream with visible timestamps rather than in a conventional grid.
"Swiss Minimalism" is a design aesthetic that emphasises readability, high contrast typeface, and extensive use of whitespace to draw the reader's attention to the author's words.
Discovery Feature: An archive option called "This Time Yesterday" lets viewers go back precisely 24 hours in the feed to see what they missed.

3. Assumptions
Public Access: Although publishing and interacting with the Global Feed require an account, we anticipate that it is accessible to the public without one.
Tagging Restrictions: In order to preserve the integrity of the worldwide search and filtering system, we presume that authors are only allowed to use five category tags per post.

4. List of Features
User authentication: Authors can securely register and log in to manage their posts and profiles.
Article CRUD Engine: A powerful text editor with markdown support that lets writers create, read, modify, and delete articles.
Global Chronological Feed: The homepage engine that uses the published_at date to get and present posts in a strict descending sequence.
Responsive Reader Mode: An immersive long-form reading experience by removing UI clutter in a mobile-optimized layout.
A taxonomy tool called the "Category Tagging System" enables authors to classify information, which viewers can then use to filter the global feed.

5. Client Questions
Rate Limiting: Should we put in place "Cooldown" periods for writers to stop one person from posting a lot of content quickly on the global chronological feed?
Pinned Content: Is it necessary for the feed to be strictly chronological, or does the Board want the option to "Pin" a particular announcement or featured piece to the top?
Content Lifecycle: In order to maintain the speed of the main feed, should there be a "Archive" feature that moves posts older than a specific age (such as a year) to a different search index?
Bot Prevention: How much automated "Spam Filtering" is necessary for the MVP to guarantee the feed stays high-quality given its public nature?
